# Practice Email Examples

These are fictional examples created for this project. There are no working links or real organizations.

## Example 1: Urgent account warning

**From:** Campus IT Support `<support@campus-security.example>`  
**Subject:** Your student account will close today

> We found a problem with your student account. Confirm your password immediately using the link below or your account will be disabled.

### What stood out

- The message creates a deadline and tries to cause fear.
- The sender address may not match the school's real domain.
- It asks the reader to confirm a password.
- It pushes the reader toward an unexpected link.

**Decision:** This has several common phishing signs.

**Safe response:** Do not use the link. Open the school's official portal separately or contact the real IT help desk using contact information from the school website.

## Example 2: Fake delivery fee

**From:** Package Updates `<delivery@parcel-alert.example>`  
**Subject:** Small delivery fee required

> We could not deliver your package. Pay a $2.00 fee now to prevent it from being returned.

### What stood out

- The message is unexpected.
- It uses a small payment to make the request seem harmless.
- It creates urgency by threatening to return the package.
- The sender does not clearly identify a real delivery company.

**Decision:** The message is suspicious and should not be trusted.

**Safe response:** Do not use the message link. Check any expected delivery through the seller's website or the delivery company's official tracking page.

## Example 3: Class update

**From:** Professor Reed `<reed@college.example>`  
**Subject:** Reading posted for Thursday's class

> I uploaded the reading for Thursday to the class learning portal. We will discuss pages 10–18 during class.

### What stood out

- The message does not ask for a password, payment, or personal information.
- It does not pressure the reader to act immediately.
- It tells the student to use the normal class portal instead of an unfamiliar link.
- The topic would make sense if the student is enrolled in the class.

**Decision:** This example appears less suspicious, but the reader should still consider whether the message was expected and whether the sender address is correct.

**Safe response:** Open the learning portal through the normal school website instead of using an unexpected link.

## Comparison

## My conclusion

The first two messages try to create a fast emotional reaction. The third message fits a normal situation and does not ask for anything sensitive. This comparison showed me why context matters when deciding whether an email is safe.
