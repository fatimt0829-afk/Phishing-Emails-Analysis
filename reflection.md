# Project Reflection

I wanted this project to be based on something I had actually experienced, so I used two suspicious emails from my own inbox instead of only using made-up examples.

The first email tried to scare me by saying that my photos would be deleted. The second tried to attract me with money. Seeing them next to each other helped me understand that phishing is not one specific type of message. The topic can change, but the goal is still to influence the reader's decision.

The biggest lesson I learned was to pay attention to the emotion an email is creating. If a message makes me feel panicked, excited, or rushed, that is a reason to slow down and check it more carefully. I should look at the sender, decide whether the request makes sense, and verify it through the real app or website.

I also learned not to rely only on spelling mistakes. The Venmo message looked cleaner than the storage warning, but the blank greeting, missing amount, unclear sender, and request to answer questions were still suspicious.

If I received either message again, I would avoid the buttons, check the real account separately, report the email as phishing, and delete it. This project helped me practice phishing awareness, recognizing social engineering tactics, and explaining risk in clear language.
